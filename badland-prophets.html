Badland Prophets

Designer, writer, layout · 2026

A Powered by the Apocalypse western set inside a sentient dust storm.

  • Systems design
  • PbtA
  • Narrative design
  • Layout

Premise

A storm covers the planet. The only livable ground is inside its eyes, mile-wide calms that drift across the land carrying caravan towns with them. Prophets work the edges, salvaging whatever the storm gave back.

What it gives back is changed. Coal that won't burn. A pickaxe that shatters into gems. A revolver that heals the people it shoots. Whether the storm intends any of it is left to the table.

Thesis: community versus survival. Five stats: EYE, IRON, SILVER, GRIT, CREED.

The Ranger

None of the four features add damage. Official Documentation opens a deal once per scene. Piece by Piece turns a partial success into questions the player gets to ask. Staredown lets the Ranger intimidate off GRIT instead of SILVER. Quickdraw handles initiative and ambushes, breaking ties on IRON. Two of them cost exhaustion to push further.

Rules I'd point at

Oaths bind without a roll

Make a promise with your hand on something that matters and it holds. Keep it, take a bonus on anything in its service. Break it and the wronged party knows immediately. The storm keeps accounts. Nobody knows how.

A missed prophecy is still true

On the worst result of the divination move you still get a vision. The GM decides what it's about and it comes true sideways. Write it down. Misses generate foreshadowing the fiction now owes.

Duels run on declared stakes

Both sides say aloud what they're fighting for, then one roll settles it. NPCs never roll. The declaration is what a partial success takes away from you.

Harm is a condition with a cure

No hit points. Winged, Shaken, Parched, Disgraced, Marked. Each penalizes one kind of move and names what clears it. Marked has no known cure.

Twelve playbooks, each owning one verb nobody else has. The Dowser reads the storm, the Reclaimer values what comes out, the Coyote crosses it, the Undertaker buries it. Moves that drift onto another playbook's verb get cut.
